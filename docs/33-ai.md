# 33 - The shipped AI: what its builtins answer, and why the robots did not fight

The game's AI is its own Python (`AI/__init__.py` `SuperAI`, `AI/Tactics.py`, one
brain per bot from `AI/Bindings.py`) running on top of C++ builtins: the
`plus.AI` methods and the `plus.Arena` navigation. We run the shipped scripts
unmodified (docs/26 §AI gap); this document records what the builtins do in
`Robot Arena 2.exe` and what was wrong in our answers. Everything here was read
in Ghidra/rizin (REA) or measured on the live captures in
`local/oracle/original/`; nothing was transliterated.

Symptoms before (fidelity `6c9c3f2`, havok17): Box Scout-ALARM 59 s of pushing
without damage; Tornado-Ripblade still after 2 s; Compressor Sentinel-Wide Load
never left their starts; nearly every match ended on LastRobot (immobilised 20 s)
with 0 points. Five independent faults, in the order they bit.

## 1. Binding table (`0x4bba00`, `0x4b7750`, `0x4b6000`, `0x4cd6b0`)

`FUN_004bba00` registers the whole Python surface (Boost.Python style); three
more registrars it calls add the sounds (`0x4b7750`), the particle emitter and
`rayTest` (`0x4b6000`) and the per-player `plus` functions (`0x4cd6b0`). Every
method of the shipped scripts' surface, with the exe's wrapper, what it calls,
and the state of ours after the audit of 2026-11-02 (docs/29 §12an). Addresses
in the second column are the Python-callable wrapper, then the member it calls.

Status: **V** read in the binary *and* the original's answer observed live
(`ainative-scout-alarm-box__live__000/001` logs every native call the brains
make; `ainprobe-scout-alarm-box__live__000` is the probe of the edge cases);
**A** read in the binary, ours agrees, not observed separately; **F** a
mismatch found and fixed (section 8); **E** an edge case that still differs
(why); **U** no shipped script calls it (ours absent or a stub); **X** ours only,
the exe has no such name (the shipped scripts define it in Python, or never call it).

## 2. Navigation: collision lines and POVs, not a grid search

Our `GetPath` was an A* over the `.map` height grid with a wall-clearance
square of `ceil(radius / resolution)` cells and `AddCollisionLine` rasterised into
walls. A robot within two cells of a wall (`radius` 1.2-1.4 in `Bindings.py`)
had no passable neighbour, so `GetPath` returned `[]`, `DriveToLocation` drove
nowhere and the robot stayed parked until the 20 s immobility rule ended the
match. The original never consults the grid for routing:

* The "grid" of the scripts is the world plane: `ToGrid(p)` is `(p.x, p.z)`
  (`0x406240` just builds a 2-vector) and `FromGrid((x, z))` is
  `(x, floor height, z)`. `Bridge.py`'s override returns `(self.ToGrid(end),)` as
  a one-waypoint path, which only works this way.
* `0x4058b0(point, radius)` tests the circle against every `AddCollisionLine`
  segment (`0x61b260`: the line's two crossing parameters, a hit when one lies
  in `[0, 1]`; a segment wholly inside the circle does not count).
* `IsStraightPathClear` (`0x405720`) marches from the start in steps of
  `radius * 0.5` (`0x6a55f0` = 0.5), `trunc(length / step)` stops, end point not
  tested, and fails at the first stop that hits a line.
* `GetPath` (`0x405990`), recovered in full and checked on every one of 5179
  live calls (`boxfight-scout-alarm-box__live__000..005`, docs/29 §12ab, and
  `boxstart-scout-alarm-box__live__000..004`, docs/29 §12ah). **Which call is
  which** (§12ah): the script's `Arena.GetPath(from_world, to_world)` is
  `0x4ba370`; it calls the native one with `p1 = from_world`, `p2 = to_world`
  and *reverses* the native list, so the script sees `[from, ..., to']`. The
  native search: the straight test marches from `p1` toward `p2`; clear, the
  list is `[p2, p1]`. Otherwise a `p2` that hits a line is moved to the first
  clear stop of its march toward the first vantage point that has one
  (`0x405c30`); the same is attempted for `p1` but the found point lands in
  the vantage point's temporary, so `p1` never moves. The list is an A*
  (`0x4067b0`/`0x407330`, at most 1000 expansions) from `p1` to the moved
  `p2` over the `AddPOV` points cut to whole numbers, stepping over each
  point's own neighbour list, with *squared* distances for the steps and the
  estimate (`0x407890`); a free point's successors are the points its march
  reaches, a vantage point's are `p2` (when its march reaches it) and its
  neighbours; the open list's binary heap (erase, `make_heap`, `push_heap`
  of the MSVC library, `0x40f6d0`, `0x411cf0`, `0x411c50`) settles ties. When
  the A* fails the list is `[moved p2, p1]`. The search is not symmetric, so
  who asks matters: at the start of a box fight the Scout is told
  `[Scout, (0,1), (5,-1), (8,-5), ALARM]` and ALARM `[ALARM, (5,5), (0,10), Scout]`.

Before (§12ab), ours marched from `from`, moved a blocked `to` as well, used
the POVs' exact locations and plain distances (Dijkstra): it agreed with live
on 1238 of 1434 calls of one fight and 183 of 342 of another. Most of the
misses are near the hazards: with the enemy pressed against a spike's box live
drives straight at it, ours went round over a vantage point (to a moved goal)
and came at it from the other side.

`AddCollisionLine` only records the segment. The grid stays for `DistanceToEdge`
(tabletops) and floor heights.

## 3. The damage records are 5-tuples

`GetLastDamageReceived` reads robot `+0xd8`, `GetLastDamageDone` `+0xc4`: `+0`
component, `+4` amount, `+8` the other player, `+0xc` the other player's
component, `+0x10` the time, built into the tuple as `(+0, +4, +0x10, +8, +0xc)`.
`AI/Spinner.py`'s `RobotInRange` reads `damage[3]` ("by player"); our 3-tuple
raised `IndexError` inside `Engage` for every spinner once an enemy came within
`spin_range`, the brain's `Tick` failed every eighth of a second, and the robot
kept its last command. For a blow *done*, `component` is the attacker's own part
(`CheckForWeaponHits` matches it against `self.weapons`), so the simulator now
records the attacker's part (`combat::Impact::by`) — before, it stored the
victim's. "No hit yet" is `(0, 0.0, 0.0, -1, -1)`.

## 4. The robots' own SmartZones

`Poker`, `Chopper`, `Flipper`, `Whipper` fire only when `self.sensors` holds an
enemy, and the sensors are fed by the robot's SmartZone component:
`RegisterSmartZone(self.zone, 1)` in `Activate`, `SmartZoneEvent(direction, id,
robot + 1, chassis)` per body entering/leaving, `GetZoneContents(self.zone)`
once a second in `CheckSensors`. We had no robot zones at all — `GetZoneContents`
answered from the *arena's* zones — so no weapon brain ever pressed its trigger.

The zone is a SmartZone component in the `.bot` (`SmartZone` /
`Components\smartzone1.txt` / its name line `weapon`, `flip`, `swing`), whose
definition is a box mesh `smart_zone` (`smartzone1.gmf`: 0.44 x 0.29 x 0.46 m,
1.06-1.52 m out from its attach point; `Styles\smartzone2/3` are larger). The
renderer skips these components; `ra2_render::bot_smart_zones` now loads their
meshes and mounts them with the same assembly rule as every other component, and
`rig::SmartZone` carries the box on the zone component's part. Each step the duel
tests every attached part of every *other* live robot against each registered
zone (`watch_brain_zones`), publishes the readings for `GetZoneContents`, and
sends `SmartZoneEvent` for each part that entered or left. The robot's own parts
are not reported: the shipped brains fire whenever their table holds anything,
and in the original weapon test ALARM idled rather than firing continuously.

The other notifications now reach the brains: `LostComponent(id)` to the robot
whose part came off (with `FractureEvent` to all), `PlayerOut(id)` when a robot
is out. `plus.AI` has the base versions the brains call up into
(`AI.SuperAI.LostComponent(self, id)` in every weapon brain), each answering
`True`.

## 5. The robots faced backwards and turned the wrong way

Two measurements from the original:

* `assembly-scout__live__000`: at the first step the chassis' `+Z` points at the
  arena centre and the flipper's burst motor sits at chassis `(-0.01, 0.21,
  0.33)`, in front. `GetHeading` (`0x4df320`) is `(0,0,1)` turned by the `.bot`
  heading: a robot's front is its chassis `+Z` turned by the heading, and the
  Razor (heading `pi`) runs at 16.4 m/s along its chassis `-Z` in
  `magic-razor__live__000`.
* `magic-razor__live__000`: with `SuperAI.Turn(+100)` every wheel record reads
  thrust -1940, the right-hand wheels' record axle is `(-1, 0, 0)`, and the
  chassis spins at +12.7 rad/s about `+Y`: `Turn(+)` *raises* the heading. The
  aim leg, steered by the game's own `AimToHeading`, converges with positive
  commands (heading 0.76 -> 1.44 -> 2.21 at `cmd_turn` 100, 42, 11).

Ours had the opposite convention throughout, consistently enough that straight
driving looked right: `RobotRig::forward` was `-Z` turned by the heading, the
`MagicAction` record axle was the bare hinge axis (`+X` for the same wheel), and
the spawn yaw put `-Z` toward the centre. The robots therefore drove toward their
own back, started with their weapons (and now their SmartZones) facing away,
and turned at -3.7 rad/s for `Turn(+100)`. The previous fix inverted the AI's
`LeftRight` channel, which made them steer but still fight backwards. Now:

* `RobotRig::forward` = `(sin h, 0, cos h)` (the original's `GetHeading` vector);
* the record axle is `CLOCKWISE * axis` (the motor step's `-direction`,
  `0x481450`), the same as the live records; the interim (Jolt) wheel push uses
  `axle x normal` to agree with it;
* the spawn yaw turns that front toward the centre (`BotSetup::yaw_deg` keeps
  its meaning: the front is `-Z` turned by it);
* the stand-in driver's steer is no longer negated (a positive steer, the `D`
  key, raises the heading like `Turn(+)`);
* the AI command path has no mirror.

Weapon spin directions are untouched (`CLOCKWISE` still applies to hinge motors).

## 8. The audit of every native the scripts call (docs/29 §12an)

Method: the registrars' string tables were read for every name and its wrapper,
each wrapper's member was read in the binary, and one live session (the Scout v
ALARM fight in the Combat Arena, `:7`) logged every native query of the brains
for 100 s (`ainative-...__live__000`, 29 670 calls) and answered about 130 probes of
edge cases (`ainprobe-...__live__000`). Five mismatches were found and fixed,
three smaller ones and a few edge cases are recorded in section 1.

* **`GetDirection` and `GetSpeed` were flattened.** `0x4df3d0` turns `(0,0,1)`
  by the heading and then by the chassis body and hands it back; ours projected
  it onto the floor and re-normalised, and `GetSpeed` (`0x401c90`:
  `GetDirection . linear velocity`) dotted that. Live a tilted robot's direction
  has a `y` (0.3-0.6 for both robots during most of the first minute, e.g.
  `(0.878, 0.474, 0.061)`), and on the 733 (ALARM) and 699 (Scout) `GetSpeed` samples with the
  location and direction logged alongside, the speed matches the velocity from
  the location differences (0.125 s apart) along the raw direction better than
  along the flattened one (rms residual 0.289 against 0.299 and 0.347 against
  0.356 over all samples; on the samples with `|dir.y| > 0.3`, 148 of ALARM's
  and 18 of the Scout's, 0.18 against 0.22 and 0.23 against 0.41;
  `tools/oracle/ainative_speed.py`). `GetHeading` is the same angle either way. Now
  `ra2sim::scriptai::facing` keeps the vector and dots it.
* **`GetEnemies` ignored teams.** `0x418ff0` walks the players, skips those with
  a non-zero state (`+0x34`) or the flag `+0x38`, and keeps those `IsEnemy`
  (`0x418f40`) accepts: in a TEAM MATCH another team (`+0x30`), otherwise
  another id. Ours listed a team mate in a 2 v 2. `IsEnemy` is added (the exe
  has it; no script calls it). The flag `+0x38` is the 20 s immobility mark:
  live the opponent vanishes from the other brain's `GetEnemies` at 28.1 s,
  while the immobilised robot's own list still has its enemy. That is what
  `Robot::is_alive` already said.
* **`plus.isDefeated(unknown id)`** is 1 (ours 0), **`plus.getLocation(unknown
  id)`** is `(None, None, None)` (ours `(0, 0, 0)`), **`plus.isHuman`** asked the
  robot (ours always false: a person's robot was treated as a brain's by
  `GetNearestEnemy`'s human preference).
* **`GetInputStatus`** returns an int, truncated toward zero (17 values
  probed), and 0 for a name that is no control of the board.
* **`GetComponentHealth`** is a fraction (health over hitpoints); ours gave the
  hitpoints.
* **`Arena.RayTest`** answers `(0, (None, None, None))` for a miss (ours
  `(False, (0, 0, 0))`); **`Arena.GetMapSize`** was missing.
* **`plus.rayTest`** (the flames): a miss is five `-1`s (ours put the ray's
  start in the point), and **the arena's walls and floor strike first**: live a
  ray from outside the Combat Arena's wall to a robot inside returns
  `(-1, -1, -12.7, y, z)`, from below the floor `(-1, -1, -1, -1, -1)`. Ours
  tested only the robots' parts, so a jet behind a wall could scorch the robot
  on the other side. The shipped flames (Bridge, clawtop, hilltop: 16 jets) were run through
  ours at 0.25, 0.5 and 1 s: only the Bridge's four, which point down at the
  deck, are cut, at the deck surface (y = -0.72), below any robot standing on it.

Not changed: `GetInputStatus` for a *switch* control (see section 1), the
`GetStartPointLocation` index past the end (live reads garbage), a negative id
(live `ValueError`), `GetComponentType(99)` (live hangs the exec), the record
before any hit, and a `rayTest` that starts inside a part.
